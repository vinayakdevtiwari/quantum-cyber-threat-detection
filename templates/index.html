QDS LIVE — INTERCEPTION ON A QUANTUM CHANNEL
Group room over a quantum digital signature link. Open on two devices.

1 — Open the room

A WhatsApp-style channel. Many participants can talk, and every message spends a fixed transit window in the channel with its bits exposed. That window is the entire attack surface.

2 — Open the attack console

Runs on a second device, at its own address, so participants cannot see the inject button. Enter the room code, then act while a message is in transit.

The demo, in four steps

1. The room gives you a 4-letter code.
2. Open the console on device 2 with that code.
3. Send a message — everyone sees transmitting… and a countdown. No plaintext appears before delivery, not even to the sender.
4. The console turns amber. Inject Replace text before the window closes. The room receives the substituted message, flagged TAMPERED, then learns who attacked.

What it checks

Per-bit integrity catches corruption. The quantum witness catches substitution. Context binding catches replay — a genuine earlier signature passes every quantum check and is stopped only because it is bound to a stale session.

Six attacks: bit-flip forgery, forgery by substitution, replay, impersonation, channel manipulation, and an honest control.

Before you start

Both devices must be on the same network as this laptop. Open the laptop's LAN address — for example http://192.168.137.1:5000/room — not localhost. If Windows asks about the firewall, choose Allow on private networks.

Your laptop runs the quantum simulation (Qiskit). Phones are remote controls that send instructions and render results — quantum processing cannot run on a phone.

Analytics dashboard

On the laptop at http://localhost:8501. Room messages archive to the same database it reads, so an attack injected from a phone appears in the SOC view with no manual step.

Single-shot fallback

For a simpler two-screen run: victim and attacker. Same quantum backend, one message at a time.
